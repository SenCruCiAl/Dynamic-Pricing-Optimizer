01Method

Demand has constant elasticity e with multiplicative noise, so log demand is linear in log price:

D(p) = A · (p / p₀)^(−e) · exp(ε), ε ~ N(0, σ²)

The fitted model is ordinary least squares on log demand against log price. Profit is (p − c) · D(p). Setting its derivative to zero gives the Lerner markup, which is the ground truth every chosen price is scored against:

p* = c · e / (e − 1) for e > 1, clipped to the allowed range

Two consequences are easy to see with the sliders. With zero cost and elastic demand, revenue falls at every price, so the best price is always the floor. With inelastic demand (e ≤ 1), profit rises with price without limit, so the answer is always the ceiling. Neither is a pricing insight; they are corners of the model.

The optimiser does a dense grid search, then refines the best bracket with Brent's method. Brent's method alone assumes a single peak, which fails on the step-shaped curves a random forest produces.
